Project Guidelines

What is a "good" project?

There are a few things to consider about your idea before even starting your project:

  • Is it something you would want or use?
  • Is it original and made by you?
    • If it uses a tutorial, did you change it significantly enough that it could be considered your design and not a copy of the tutorial's?
  • Would other people want this project?

If you find that your idea doesn't fit these criteria or the rest of the criteria on this page, rethink it or come up with something that fits.

Functionality

Your project doesn't have to be incredibly complicated, it just has to serve a purpose. When coming up with your next project, think about what you would use and find useful and/or enjoyable. The things you create during Atlantis need to have a purpose, and that purpose does not include being looked at. You can and should make good looking projects, but they all need to actually do something.

A great example of simple but useful (and cute) is this low poly dino doorstop — it has both a use, and looks nice.

If you put as your project's purpose or use case something like "to be put on display and admired" it will not be accepted 99% of the time. If it is, for example, a toy, it can't just be a static model with no moving parts. A toy car could have wheels and doors that open, both of which are not very difficult to implement.

We encourage mechanical projects, meaning something with parts that move and add functionality to the project. If your project has mechanical aspects, it is much more likely to be accepted. That doesn't mean you should try and fit one in wherever you can — still thoughtfully plan out what you are going to make before making it, and before each drastic change you plan on making.

Printability

Not every part of your project has to be 3D printed, but most of it should be. Atlantis is all about 3D printing — after all, the reward is a 3D printer. We have a wonderful guide made by the lovely Parker Rupe here on the website that guides you through making parts that are 3D printable and able to be made real.

Presentation and polish

This applies not just to the CAD, but to the Printables page you will have to create for each project you make. The project should look like it belongs on the front page of Printables; it should be something you are proud of and want to show the world. Every project must also be under an open source license.

Chamfers and fillets can make rough edges and boxy designs look much better. Using them to polish your models is a good habit to get into — just make sure not to ruin a part's function by stylizing.

What you shouldn't timelapse

All CAD should be recorded with Lookout, however some things shouldn't be recorded.

Do not record or log time for:

• Watching tutorial videos
• Printing your project

You can record yourself slicing the project to check for printability, but not for actually printing it. Don't abuse this ability.

Specifically disallowed projects

These rules are enforced by Hack Club as a whole and not by Atlantis specifically.

  • Weapons (real or prop)
  • Anything for sponsored robotics teams such as FRC, FTC, and VEX
  • Anything NSFW
